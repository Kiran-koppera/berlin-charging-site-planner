3 · Shortlist

Score = 35 % charging gap of the area + 25 % traffic on the adjacent road + 25 % distance from existing HPC + 15 % host fit (dwell time). Sites are at least 1.5 km apart. Forecourts of fuel brands that run their own HPC network are excluded. Candidate hosts come from OpenStreetMap; no business has been contacted. CSV

9 · Method, assumptions and limits

Method

  • Supply: Bundesnetzagentur charging register, cleaned and grouped into sites; AC < 50 kW, DC 50–149 kW, HPC ≥ 150 kW.
  • Demand: residents per planning area × BEVs per resident (KBA) × daily kWh × share of charging that must be public (60 % inner city, 35 % outer city) + through traffic from 2023 traffic counts.
  • Coverage: residents spread over a 250 m grid; covered if within 300 m of AC or 1.5 km of HPC.
  • Sites: 4,800+ candidate hosts from OpenStreetMap scored and picked greedily with 1.5 km spacing.
  • Forecast: stock-and-flow model (Euler, quarter-year step) with a reinforcing availability loop and balancing saturation and build-out loops, calibrated to KBA data.
  • Validation: out-of-time backtest and a logistic-regression model of where operators opened HPC.

Limits

  • Only devices that completed the register notification are included; real supply is somewhat larger.
  • Demand counts Berlin-registered cars; fleets and ride-hailing cars registered elsewhere add demand, so city-wide utilisation is understated. The relative gap is unaffected.
  • Population is spread evenly within each planning area; road counts cover the main network only.
  • No grid-capacity data: medium-voltage availability decides many sites and must be checked with the grid operator.
  • Costs and prices are planning assumptions with a stated basis, not supplier quotes.
